Complete Metabolic Panel (CMP): Understanding All 14 Tests in Your Blood Work

What is a Complete Metabolic Panel?

A Complete Metabolic Panel (CMP) is a group of 14 blood tests that gives your doctor a comprehensive overview of your body's chemistry and metabolism. It is one of the most frequently ordered laboratory tests in medicine, used for:

- Routine annual health check-ups

- Monitoring chronic conditions (diabetes, kidney disease, liver disease)

- Evaluating symptoms (fatigue, weakness, nausea)

- Before surgery or starting new medications

- Emergency evaluation

The CMP is an expansion of the Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP), adding liver function tests to the kidney and electrolyte tests.

Glucose — Your Blood Sugar

Fasting normal range: 70-99 mg/dL

Random (non-fasting) normal: <140 mg/dL

Glucose is the primary energy source for every cell in your body. In a CMP, glucose reflects your blood sugar at the time of the blood draw.

Interpretation:

- Below 70 mg/dL: Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar) — can cause dizziness, sweating, confusion

- 70-99 mg/dL: Normal fasting glucose

- 100-125 mg/dL: Impaired fasting glucose (prediabetes)

- 126+ mg/dL (on two occasions): Diabetes mellitus

- Very high (>400 mg/dL): Can indicate diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar state

Electrolytes — The Four Essential Minerals

Sodium (Na+): Normal 136-145 mEq/L

Regulates fluid balance. Low sodium (hyponatremia) causes confusion, headache. High sodium (hypernatremia) causes thirst, confusion, seizures.

Potassium (K+): Normal 3.5-5.0 mEq/L

Critical for heart rhythm. Both low (hypokalemia) and high (hyperkalemia) can cause dangerous cardiac arrhythmias.

Carbon Dioxide (CO2/Bicarbonate): Normal 22-29 mEq/L

Reflects the body's acid-base balance. Low CO2 suggests acidosis; high CO2 suggests alkalosis.

Chloride (Cl-): Normal 98-106 mEq/L

Works with sodium to maintain fluid balance. Usually interpreted alongside sodium and CO2.

Kidney Markers — BUN and Creatinine

Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN): Normal 7-20 mg/dL

Urea is a waste product from protein metabolism. BUN rises when kidneys aren't filtering well, but also with dehydration, high protein diet, or gastrointestinal bleeding.

Creatinine: Normal 0.7-1.2 mg/dL (men), 0.5-1.0 mg/dL (women)

Muscle waste product — the most reliable kidney function marker. Rises when kidney filtration decreases.

BUN:Creatinine Ratio:

- Normal: 10:1 to 20:1

- High ratio (>20): Dehydration, heart failure, GI bleeding

- Low ratio (<10): Liver disease, malnutrition

Calcium: Normal 8.5-10.2 mg/dL

Regulates bone health, muscle function, nerve signaling, blood clotting. Abnormal calcium can indicate parathyroid problems, vitamin D deficiency, or malignancy.

How to Prepare for a CMP

Fasting: Fast for 8-12 hours before the test. You may drink water. This gives the most accurate glucose and other values.

Medications: Continue all regular medications unless your doctor specifically says otherwise.

Exercise: Avoid intense exercise 24 hours before, as it can temporarily raise potassium, AST, and creatinine.

Hydration: Stay normally hydrated. Dehydration can falsely elevate creatinine, BUN, and sodium.

Timing: Morning blood draws are preferred for fasting tests.

Discuss: Always tell your doctor all medications, supplements, and herbal products you take, as many can affect CMP values.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long does CMP take to come back?

Most hospital labs report CMP results within 24 hours. Many return results in 4-8 hours for urgent cases. Point-of-care analyzers can give results in minutes.

Q: What's the difference between CMP and BMP?

The Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP) has 8 tests: glucose, calcium, electrolytes (Na, K, CO2, Cl), BUN, and creatinine. The CMP adds 6 liver function tests: ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin, albumin, and total protein.

Q: Can I drink coffee before a CMP?

It's best to avoid coffee before a fasting CMP. Black coffee can affect glucose levels and may interfere with certain tests. Stick to plain water.
